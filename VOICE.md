# Voice Profile — vats (Clear & Muddy)

Derived from three Substack essays (full texts in `voice-samples/`). **Read the
warning first: this profile is a calibration reference, not an imitation target.**

## The register dial (the most important thing in this file)

The Substack essays are personal essays - self-interrogating, chatty, anecdotal.
Explorables are a different genre. The author wants them **mostly neutral**, closer
to explanatory magazine prose, with the humanity showing through in a few controlled
places. Aim for roughly:

```
neutral textbook ---------|--------X----------------- Substack essays
                          explorables live here
```

**Failure mode (confirmed by the author on a real draft): stacking their verbal tics
("if you are anything like me", question volleys, chatty asides) reads as a bad
impression, not as their voice.** One touch per few paragraphs, not per sentence.
When unsure, write neutral - the author edits warmth in more easily than they can
scrub cringe out.

## What DOES carry into explorables (dialed low)

- **Concrete openings.** Start inside a moment or a case, never with throat-clearing.
  ("A person discovers a mole on her leg. She Googles it.")
- **An occasional direct question to the reader**, where the reader genuinely has an
  answer ("When you read "2,000 birds," did you picture two thousand birds?").
  At most one such moment per section.
- **Honest hedging where honestly uncertain.** "Maybe." "This doesn't settle it."
  The author marks the edge of knowledge explicitly; never fake confidence.
- **A limits acknowledgment before the close** - what the featured studies don't
  explain, treated respectfully. (Their essays always have one: "This lens doesn't
  explain everything.")
- **Invitations over commands** in widget instructions and endings. "Sit with the
  question" energy, not "you should".
- **Plain vocabulary.** "a bit", "pretty much" are fine; no inflated register.
- **Spaced hyphens as dashes** ( - ) rather than em dashes, matching their
  punctuation habit.

## What does NOT carry into explorables

- Question-answer volleys ("Sure. Is that it? No.")
- Self-implicating chattiness ("if you are anything like me", "my first instinct
  was...") - authentic in their essays, parody in an explorable
- Casual slang and exclamation marks ("kinda", "scared the shit out of me", "!")
- Long personal anecdotes as evidence (explorables use studies and widgets instead)
- Parenthetical asides with personality, except very sparingly

## Reference: the full essay-voice fingerprint

Kept for essays or if the author ever wants the dial turned up. Signature moves in
the Substack samples: openings inside concrete moments; constant rhetorical
questions, headers as questions; spaced hyphens, never em dashes; parentheticals
with personality ("(she's a natural storyteller)"); "Maybe." as a full sentence;
self-implicating anecdotes before generalizing; homely bodily metaphors offered as
optional ("knot is a metaphor that works for me but feel free to come up with your
own"); a limits section; endings that invite or call back to the opening image;
closing "not X, but Y" contrasts ("Not because you didn't have enough evidence, but
because you couldn't afford to.").

## Process rule

When drafting a new piece: write neutral-first, then make ONE calibration pass
adding at most a handful of voice touches from the "carries over" list. Show the
author a sample paragraph before applying any voice treatment wholesale.
*(Learned the hard way on scope-neglect: the first voice-matched draft overdid it.)*
